## Introduction

Filing a patent in India is the process of legally protecting your invention so that no one else can make, use, sell, or import it without your permission. The Indian patent system, governed by the Patents Act, 1970 (as amended), allows protection for new products and processes in technology, pharmaceuticals, engineering, software-related inventions (with certain restrictions), and more. To qualify, your invention must be novel, involve an inventive step, and be industrially applicable. You can file as an individual, startup, company, or as a foreign applicant through an Indian patent attorney/agent. Before filing, it is crucial to conduct a prior art search to see if similar inventions already exist, and to carefully draft the patent specification, which describes the invention and defines the legal scope of protection through claims.

### The Indian patent filing process typically involves:

1. Prior Art Search & Strategy – Assess whether your invention is patentable, define what exactly to protect, and decide between provisional or complete filing.
2. Provisional or Complete Application – File a provisional application to secure an early priority date (followed by a complete specification within 12 months), or directly file a complete application if the invention is fully developed.
3. Publication – Your application is usually published after 18 months from the filing/priority date (or earlier on request through early publication).
4. Request for Examination (RFE) – File a separate request within the prescribed time so that the Patent Office will examine your application substantively.
5. Examination & Office Actions – Patent Examiner issues an examination report listing objections on novelty, inventive step, clarity, or formal issues; you respond with amendments, arguments, and attend hearings if needed.
6. Grant or Refusal – If objections are overcome, the patent is granted and published in the Patent Journal; otherwise, it may be refused with reasons.
7. Post‑Grant Maintenance – Pay annual renewal fees and enforce your patent rights (through notices, licensing, or legal action) against infringers
